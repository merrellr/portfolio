Product Advisory · Built by me · In private build

Datum: AI reporting where every number can be checked

An AI-first reporting product for companies without a data team. It is built around official metric definitions: ask in plain English and see the SQL behind the answer, and every AI summary is checked number by number against the data before anyone reads it.

Built by meAI analyticsSemantic layerAI agentsSaaS pricing
+

Challenge

Business users have questions their data could answer, but they wait on a data team or on a dashboard someone else built. AI tools now let anyone ask in plain English, yet they fall short in three ways: the cost climbs with every question, the queries aren't optimized for the data behind them, and there's no single source of truth, so the same question can get two different answers. Datum puts the questions in business users' hands with the organization's data governance built in: agreed definitions, the right permissions, and answers anyone can check.

Approach

  • One definition per metric: a metric library everyone agrees on, so "Revenue" means the same thing on every dashboard and in every answer. The AI uses these definitions first, and metrics an admin has checked are marked Verified.
  • Answers that show their work: ask in plain English and get a chart plus "How I answered": the metric used, the source, the assumptions and the SQL.
  • AI summaries that can't drift: every chart and dashboard gets a short summary, and each figure in it is checked against the data before it's shown. Each number links back to its metric.
  • Role-based agents: an executive, sales, marketing, finance, customer success or operations analyst, each set up for that role. They only see what the person asking can see.
  • Work that comes to you: goals tracked from the data, alerts when a metric crosses a line (and again when it recovers), and scheduled updates by email or Microsoft Teams.
  • Bring your own AI: a connector lets a company's own Claude or Copilot use its data, under the same definitions and permissions.
  • Separate by design: each client company's data is kept completely separate, with roles for admins, editors and viewers, and a monthly AI budget an admin controls.
  • A business model to match: reporting is the base product, AI usage is passed through close to cost, and the connector is a paid add-on.

Where it is

In design and gathering feedback from business users, with a tentative 2028 launch. The working build already has about 230 automated tests, and every page is checked at phone, tablet and laptop widths. I'll add results here once clients are using it, rather than estimate them now.

Product Advisory · 2025–26 · Built by me

AI skills assessment & learning platform, for people and teams

A self-assessment that places product managers by level and sends them to the learning that fits, with a team view for leaders and decision tools teams score together.

Built by meAI gradingLearning platformTeam analyticsCollaboration
+

Challenge

Product managers are being asked to adopt AI quickly, but a single course library doesn't fit everyone. Some PMs have never used it, while others are already advanced practitioners, and both groups waste time on content pitched at the wrong level.

Approach

  • Defined a framework of five core product competencies plus individual and organizational AI fluency, each with four tiers, from novice to advanced.
  • Built the assessment, AI grading of a free-text leadership scenario (with daily usage limits), and a course platform with login and resume-where-you-left-off.
  • For each learner: a next best step on the home page, My growth comparing their first and latest skill check area by area, badges, role targets, and every lesson read aloud.
  • For leaders: a team skills heat map (by person, or team-only for privacy) that names the biggest gap and suggests a course, assignments with due dates, and weekly or monthly email reports.
  • Decide together: the free toolkits open inside the platform and save to each person's account. A scorecard can be scored as a team: everyone scores alone, then the scores are revealed and the biggest disagreements are discussed first.
  • An owner console with funnel and lead analytics, two-step sign-in, click-through tours on every main page, and roughly 1,900 automated UI checks. Kept lean and low-cost throughout.

Outcome

The platform is live and in pilots. Learners get a personal path, leaders see where their team needs help and can act on it in one click, and teams settle prioritization debates with data instead of the loudest voice. It also shows I can take a product from idea to production myself.

Product Advisory · 2026 · Built by me

An AI learning coach that knows where you're stuck

I reimagined the learning platform around a coach. It reads the skill check, the courses and every answer, says what to do next and why, runs adaptive practice that explains each answer, and talks it through in a chat grounded in the lessons.

Built by meAI coachAdaptive practiceLearning analyticsFreemium
+

Challenge

A course catalog makes the learner do the work: decide what to take, notice what isn't sticking, and keep going. Most people stall after the first course. General AI chat doesn't fix it either: the learner has to know what to ask, and it knows nothing about their progress. I wanted the AI to run the loop, not sit beside it: Assess, find the gaps, learn, practise, understand why, then check again.

Approach

  • "Here's what I'd do next": one recommendation with the evidence behind it ("You missed 2 of your last 3 Prioritization questions. Most of the misses are questions that apply it to a situation"), a lesson ready if needed, and three alternatives.
  • Gaps by kind of question, not just topic: every check is tagged (understanding the idea, applying it to a situation, making the call, reading the evidence), so the coach can tell whether the gap is the concept or using it.
  • Adaptive practice: short sessions drawn from 300+ lesson questions. After each answer it explains why, using the lesson's own words. Two right in a row and the questions get harder; two misses and it offers to slow down or teach the idea first.
  • Readiness: each skill area is Strong, Developing or Needs attention, with the last 8 answers as dots, so progress is visible and honest. It's labelled clearly as a learning signal, not a grade.
  • The coach chat: grounded in the lessons, with citations, and in the learner's own profile; it can turn any answer into a practice session or the right lesson.
  • A business model: one free conversation, then an upgrade. Company seats include it. The limits are enforced on the server, and access is granted from an owner console.
  • The old path stays: every course is still one tab away, so nobody loses what they had.

Outcome

The platform moved from "a catalog with a quiz" to a coach that always has a reason for the next step. Every answer now feeds readiness and gaps, so recommendations sharpen the more someone practises. All of it runs on the existing stack at close to zero cost: the logic runs in the browser and is unit tested, and the chat uses a free-tier model behind daily caps.

Product Advisory · 2026 · Built by me

A site that fits each reader: read, listen or watch, your way

Every Product+ article as text, as narrated audio that highlights the words as they're spoken, and as an animated video. Visitors choose the format, the look and the level, and an AI search answers their questions with sources.

Built by meAI narrationAccessibilityPersonalizationAI search
+

Challenge

Product leaders take in ideas differently: some read at a desk, some listen on a commute, some need to see the numbers move. Text alone also leaves out people with low vision or dyslexia. Producing three formats by hand is out of reach for a small practice, so the formats had to come from one source, automatically.

Approach

  • Listen: narrated all 17 articles and 142 lessons with an open-source text-to-speech model that runs locally, so there's no cost per minute. Pronunciation rules read prices naturally, and every line is timed.
  • Read along: while it plays, the current paragraph and word are highlighted and kept in view. Tap any paragraph to listen from there. Chapters, skip, speed, resume and phone lock-screen controls come with it.
  • Watch: a video pipeline turns a storyboard, pinned to phrases in the narration, into a 1080p animated video with charts drawn from the article's own data and word-by-word captions.
  • Your way: a Read, Listen or Watch preference, so articles open straight at the player or the video. The same episodes appear in the learning platform and as a podcast on Spotify.
  • Make it yours: four complete looks, text size, density, motion and light or dark, plus "Start where you are": tell the site who's visiting (a starter, a practitioner, a leader or an executive) and it points to the right first step. Choices follow the visitor across all three Product Advisory sites.
  • Ask: an AI search that answers questions from the site's own pages, with links to its sources, and logs what it couldn't answer so the next article fills a real gap.

Outcome

Every article and lesson exists in all three formats from a single source of truth, and a new article gets its audio and video by running two scripts, at close to zero cost. Visitors meet the content in the form, look and depth that suits them, and click-through tours show first-timers how it all works.

Invitae · AI/ML · Revenue cycle

Order flows that adapt to each payer

Part of the move from giving tests away to charging for them: steer every order toward what the patient's insurance will cover.

$200M+added revenue from the organization's shift to paid testing
+

Challenge

Invitae had grown adoption by giving tests away. The organization shifted to charging for them, so every order had to fit what the patient's payer would cover, and each payer wants different clinical information, with rules that vary by policy and region.

Approach

  • Suggested the right test for each provider based on the patient's insurance, and guided them through the order flow with the documentation that payer needs.
  • Trained machine learning models on historical approvals and denials, and adjusted order workflows for each payer's policies, coverage criteria and regional differences.
  • Notified patients to switch to cash pay as soon as we could tell their insurance wouldn't cover the test.
  • Prioritized the appeals team's work on the payers most likely to approve, and moved payers unlikely to pay to cash.

Outcome

Higher reimbursement success and less administrative work for providers. Together with personalized appeal letters (below) and billing automation, this was part of the organization's shift to paid testing that added $200M+ in revenue.

Vynca · Patient & clinician experience

Guided advance directives, from first step to signature

Patients 65+ start an advance directive in their EMR's patient portal and finish it, with live or asynchronous signing for patients, providers, notaries and advocates.

73%digital ACP completion rate
~2 monthsfaster average implementation
+

Challenge

Get patients in the door, help them start an advance directive, and see it through to a signed document. The main users are 65 or older and may not be comfortable with digital tools. Most clinic laptops don't have touch screens, not every signer is ready during the visit, and health systems wanted as few physician logins as possible.

Approach

  • Start: a guided experience inside the EMR's own patient portal, with completion and review steps designed from user-centered research and behavioral health principles.

Outcome

A 73% digital completion rate for patient-centered ACP workflows, a leading result in the space, built on the same core platform as the dashboards, task manager and document review. A self-service platform cut average implementation time by about two months.
